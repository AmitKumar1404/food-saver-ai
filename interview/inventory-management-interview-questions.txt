FOODSAVER AI - INVENTORY MANAGEMENT INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
============================================================

Module:
Inventory Management

Purpose:
Interview preparation from beginner to approximately
3-year experience level.

Current implementation:

Current endpoint:

============================================================
1. BEGINNER - INVENTORY MANAGEMENT FUNDAMENTALS
============================================================

Q1. What does Inventory represent in FoodSaver AI?

Expected answer:
Inventory represents the dated quantity and lifecycle state of
a Restaurant's Product. It records how much was prepared and
how that quantity is divided into available, reserved, and
sold portions.


Q2. What is the difference between Product and Inventory?

Expected answer:
Product is a catalog or menu item with descriptive and pricing
data. Inventory is operational stock information for that
Product on a particular date.

Possible follow-up:
Can a Product exist without Inventory?

Follow-up answer:
Yes. A Restaurant can define a catalog Product before creating
dated Inventory for it.


Q3. Why should Product not contain current stock quantity?

Expected answer:
Catalog data is relatively stable, while stock changes
frequently and can vary by date. Keeping Inventory separate
avoids mixing different lifecycles and allows multiple dated
Inventory records for one Product.


Q4. Explain the Restaurant -> Product -> Inventory
relationship.

Expected answer:
A Restaurant owns Products. Inventory references both the
Restaurant and one Product belonging to that Restaurant. The
Inventory record represents that Product's quantity for one
inventory date.


Q5. Why does Inventory reference both Restaurant and Product?

Expected answer:
The Restaurant reference supports tenant or ownership scoping,
while the Product reference identifies the catalog item. The
combination also supports the one-record-per-date business key.


Q6. What is preparedQuantity?

Expected answer:
It is the total quantity initially prepared for the Inventory
record.


Q7. What is availableQuantity?

Expected answer:
It is the portion currently available for later controlled
business operations. On creation it equals preparedQuantity.


Q8. What is reservedQuantity?

Expected answer:
It is the portion allocated to reservations. It starts at zero
and is not directly controlled by the create request.


Q9. What is soldQuantity?

Expected answer:
It is the portion recorded as sold. It starts at zero and is
not directly controlled by the create request.


Q10. What quantity invariant does the module use?

Expected answer:

preparedQuantity =
availableQuantity + reservedQuantity + soldQuantity

At creation, available equals prepared while reserved and sold
are zero.


Q11. Why is BigDecimal used for quantities instead of Integer?

Expected answer:
Food quantities can be fractional, for example 0.500 kilograms
or 1.250 litres. Integer cannot represent those values without
an agreed smallest-unit conversion.


Q12. Why is BigDecimal preferred over Double?

Expected answer:
Double uses binary floating-point representation and can
produce rounding surprises for decimal values. BigDecimal
provides explicit decimal precision and scale.


============================================================
2. INTERMEDIATE - ENTITY AND DATABASE DESIGN
============================================================

Q13. What are the important Inventory entity fields?

Expected answer:
The entity contains id, publicId, restaurant, product,
preparedQuantity, availableQuantity, reservedQuantity,
soldQuantity, inventoryDate, status, version, createdAt, and
updatedAt.


Q14. Why are there separate internal and public identifiers?

Expected answer:
The Long id is efficient for database primary keys and joins.
The UUID publicId is suitable for external APIs and prevents
clients from depending on sequential internal keys.


Q15. Why is UUID used for publicId?

Expected answer:
UUIDs can be generated by the application, are non-sequential,
and have a very low collision probability. They reduce simple
identifier enumeration, although they do not replace
authorization.


Q16. Why is the internal database id not returned in
InventoryResponse?

Expected answer:
It is a persistence implementation detail. Hiding it keeps the
API contract independent from database key design and avoids
exposing predictable sequence values.


Q17. Why are createdAt and updatedAt stored?

Expected answer:
They support auditing, troubleshooting, synchronization, and
understanding when the record was created or last changed.


Q18. How are publicId and timestamps initialized?

Expected answer:
The entity uses @PrePersist to generate publicId, initialize
createdAt, and set updatedAt. @PreUpdate refreshes updatedAt.


Q19. What does @Version do?

Expected answer:
It enables JPA optimistic locking. JPA checks the expected
version during a write so a stale transaction cannot silently
overwrite a newer change.


Q20. Why use EnumType.STRING for InventoryStatus?

Expected answer:
String values are readable and do not depend on enum ordinal
positions. Reordering enum constants therefore does not change
the meaning of persisted values.


Q21. What do ACTIVE, DEPLETED, and CLOSED mean?

Expected answer:
They are inventory lifecycle states. ACTIVE represents an
active record, DEPLETED represents an exhausted quantity
lifecycle, and CLOSED represents an operationally closed
record. Detailed transition rules are not implemented yet.


Q22. Is InventoryStatus a food-safety status?

Expected answer:
No. ACTIVE, DEPLETED, and CLOSED describe Inventory lifecycle,
not whether food is safe, unsafe, spoiled, or eligible for
sale.

Possible follow-up:
Does surplus food mean unsafe food?

Follow-up answer:
No. Surplus food is not automatically unsafe, and unsafe food
must not automatically be treated as eligible for sale.


Q23. What are the Inventory foreign keys?

Expected answer:
restaurant_id references the owning Restaurant and product_id
references the Product represented by the Inventory record.
Both relationships are required.


Q24. Why are the Restaurant and Product relationships LAZY?

Expected answer:
LAZY loading avoids fetching related entities unless they are
needed. Mapping that accesses them should occur within a valid
persistence context or use an intentional fetch strategy.


Q25. What unique business key is enforced?

Expected answer:
The combination of restaurant_id, product_id, and
inventory_date is unique. A Restaurant cannot create two
Inventory rows for the same Product and date.


Q26. Why is database-level uniqueness important?

Expected answer:
Application checks improve error messages but concurrent
requests can both pass the check. The database constraint is
the final integrity boundary that prevents duplicate rows.


Q27. Does the current Inventory mapping declare a unique
constraint on public_id?

Expected answer:
No. The current mapping declares the composite
restaurant/product/date unique constraint, but not a database
unique constraint for public_id. Adding one through a reviewed
migration would be appropriate production hardening.

Possible follow-up:
Is UUID collision probability alone an integrity guarantee?

Follow-up answer:
No. Collision probability is extremely low, but a database
unique constraint provides deterministic enforcement.


Q28. Which Inventory indexes are declared?

Expected answer:
The indexes cover:
- restaurant_id and inventory_date
- restaurant_id and product_id
- restaurant_id and status

They support expected Restaurant-scoped date, Product, and
status access patterns.


============================================================
3. INTERMEDIATE - DTO, VALIDATION, AND API DESIGN
============================================================

Q29. What fields are in InventoryCreateRequest?

Expected answer:
productPublicId, preparedQuantity, and inventoryDate.


Q30. Why does InventoryCreateRequest not accept available,
reserved, or sold quantities?

Expected answer:
Those values are controlled by business logic. The service
initializes available from prepared and initializes reserved
and sold to zero, preserving the creation invariant.


Q31. What is currently in InventoryUpdateRequest?

Expected answer:
Only inventoryDate with @NotNull. No Inventory update endpoint
is currently implemented.


Q32. Why should quantity breakdown fields not be exposed for
arbitrary updates?

Expected answer:
Allowing clients to set each quantity independently could
break the invariant and bypass future reservation and sale
workflows. Controlled service operations should change them.


Q33. What fields are returned by InventoryResponse?

Expected answer:
publicId, restaurantPublicId, productPublicId, all four
quantities, inventoryDate, status, createdAt, and updatedAt.


Q34. Why should the JPA entity not be returned directly?

Expected answer:
DTOs prevent persistence details and lazy relationships from
leaking into the API. They also control writable and readable
fields and avoid exposing internal ids or versions.


Q35. Which fields must clients not control?

Expected answer:
Clients must not control id, publicId, availableQuantity,
reservedQuantity, soldQuantity, status, version, createdAt, or
updatedAt during Inventory creation.


Q36. Which Jakarta Validation annotations are used?

Expected answer:
productPublicId and inventoryDate use @NotNull.
preparedQuantity uses @NotNull and
@DecimalMin(value = "0.001").


Q37. Why is the minimum prepared quantity 0.001?

Expected answer:
It requires a positive quantity while matching the entity's
three-decimal scale. Zero and negative values are rejected.


Q38. Why validate at the API boundary?

Expected answer:
It rejects structurally invalid requests before business logic
and persistence execute, and it returns clear field-level
feedback to API clients.


Q39. What causes HTTP 400 for the create endpoint?

Expected answer:
Missing required fields or preparedQuantity below 0.001 causes
Jakarta Validation to fail. GlobalExceptionHandler returns
400 Bad Request with field-level messages.


Q40. What is the create Inventory endpoint?

Expected answer:
POST /api/v1/restaurants/{restaurantPublicId}/inventory


Q41. What does the controller accept?

Expected answer:
It accepts restaurantPublicId as a UUID path variable and a
validated InventoryCreateRequest as the JSON request body.


Q42. Why does successful creation return 201 Created?

Expected answer:
The request creates a new server-side resource, so 201 is more
precise than a general 200 response.


Q43. Why use ResponseEntity<InventoryResponse>?

Expected answer:
It makes the HTTP status and typed response body explicit. The
controller returns 201 with the created InventoryResponse.


Q44. What belongs in InventoryController?

Expected answer:
HTTP mapping, path and body binding, @Valid activation,
service delegation, status construction, and API
documentation metadata.


Q45. What should not be in InventoryController?

Expected answer:
Repository access, ownership checks, duplicate detection,
quantity initialization, transactions, or entity-to-response
mapping should not be placed there.


============================================================
4. ADVANCED - REPOSITORY, SERVICE, AND TRANSACTIONS
============================================================

Q46. What does InventoryRepository extend?

Expected answer:
JpaRepository<Inventory, Long>, which provides standard JPA
persistence operations for the Inventory entity.


Q47. What does findByPublicIdAndRestaurantId(...) provide?

Expected answer:
It finds an Inventory only when its public ID and owning
Restaurant internal ID both match. It supports
Restaurant-scoped ownership isolation.


Q48. Why can findByPublicId(...) alone be dangerous in a
multi-Restaurant platform?

Expected answer:
It ignores the Restaurant context from the URL. Without
additional authorization and ownership checks, one Restaurant
could access another Restaurant's resource.


Q49. What does
existsByRestaurantIdAndProductIdAndInventoryDate(...) do?

Expected answer:
It checks whether the composite business key already exists
before attempting to create another Inventory row.


Q50. Describe the createInventory service flow.

Expected answer:
The service resolves the Restaurant, resolves the Product with
Restaurant ownership, checks for duplicate Inventory, creates
the entity, initializes quantities and ACTIVE status, saves it,
and maps the saved entity to InventoryResponse.


Q51. How is Product ownership validated?

Expected answer:
The service calls
findByPublicIdAndRestaurantId(productPublicId, restaurantId).
A Product owned by another Restaurant produces no result.


Q52. How are quantities initialized during creation?

Expected answer:
prepared and available are set to the request's prepared
quantity. Reserved and sold are set to BigDecimal.ZERO.


Q53. Why is response mapping performed in the service?

Expected answer:
The service owns the application operation and API-facing
result construction. Mapping there also safely accesses lazy
Restaurant and Product references inside the transaction.


Q54. Why is createInventory annotated with @Transactional?

Expected answer:
Restaurant lookup, Product ownership validation, duplicate
checking, save, and mapping form one business operation. They
should succeed or fail as one transaction.


Q55. What could go wrong without a transaction?

Expected answer:
The operation could observe inconsistent data, partially
complete, or access lazy relationships after the persistence
context closes. Future multi-step quantity changes would be
especially vulnerable.


Q56. Why is the service layer the transaction boundary?

Expected answer:
The service coordinates the complete business use case across
repositories. A repository method covers only one persistence
operation, while a controller should remain focused on HTTP.


Q57. Which exceptions can createInventory throw?

Expected answer:
RestaurantNotFoundException, ProductNotFoundException, and
InventoryAlreadyExistsException.


Q58. How are those exceptions translated to HTTP responses?

Expected answer:
GlobalExceptionHandler maps Restaurant and Product not found
to 404 and duplicate Inventory to 409. Validation failures map
to 400 using the shared ErrorResponse structure.


Q59. What does ErrorResponse contain?

Expected answer:
It contains timestamp, numeric status, safe message, request
path, and a map of detailed errors.


Q60. Why is duplicate Inventory a 409 Conflict?

Expected answer:
The request is structurally valid, but it conflicts with an
existing resource identified by the same Restaurant, Product,
and inventory date.


============================================================
5. ADVANCED - CONCURRENCY AND PRODUCTION DESIGN
============================================================

Q61. What happens if two requests create the same Inventory
simultaneously?

Expected answer:
Both can theoretically pass the application existence check.
The database composite unique constraint must reject one
insert. The application should translate the resulting
constraint violation consistently in a production design.


Q62. Why is application duplicate checking alone insufficient?

Expected answer:
The check and insert are separate operations. Another
transaction can insert the same business key between them,
creating a race condition.


Q63. Why keep the application check if the database constraint
already exists?

Expected answer:
It provides an early, domain-specific error in the common
case. The database constraint still protects integrity under
concurrency and non-application writes.


Q64. What does optimistic locking protect against?

Expected answer:
It protects an existing Inventory row from lost updates when
two transactions read the same version and both attempt to
modify it.


Q65. Does @Version prevent duplicate creates?

Expected answer:
No. @Version protects updates or deletes of the same existing
row. The composite unique constraint protects duplicate
creation.


Q66. What happens when two users update the same Inventory?

Expected answer:
With optimistic locking, the first committed update changes
the version. The second stale write should fail with an
optimistic-lock exception instead of overwriting it silently.


Q67. What concurrency concerns will reservation and sale
operations introduce?

Expected answer:
They must prevent overselling, preserve non-negative
quantities, maintain the quantity invariant, define retry or
conflict behavior, and handle idempotency for repeated
requests.


Q68. Would read-check-write logic alone prevent overselling?

Expected answer:
Not reliably under concurrency. The design needs transactional
writes with optimistic or appropriate database locking and
clear conflict handling.


Q69. Why should Inventory initially avoid hard delete?

Expected answer:
Inventory can become part of audit, offer, reservation, and
order history. Removing it may break references or erase
business evidence. No delete API is currently implemented.


Q70. What production improvements are still needed?

Expected answer:
Examples include migrations, automated service and repository
tests, explicit units of measure, controlled quantity
operations, authorization, audit history, idempotency,
constraint-violation mapping, and optimistic-lock conflict
handling.


============================================================
6. SCENARIO-BASED QUESTIONS
============================================================

Q71. Two Restaurants have Products with the same name. Is
that a problem?

Expected answer:
No. Product names are not ownership identifiers. Inventory is
linked through the specific Product and Restaurant ids, and
ownership-aware queries keep the records isolated.


Q72. The same Product receives two create requests for the
same date. What should happen?

Expected answer:
The first request creates the record. The second should return
409 Conflict, and the database unique constraint must prevent
a duplicate even if requests race.


Q73. A client sends reservedQuantity and soldQuantity in the
create JSON. Should those values control persistence?

Expected answer:
No. InventoryCreateRequest does not expose those fields.
Unknown-property handling depends on Jackson configuration,
but the service always initializes both values to zero.


Q74. A Product belongs to Restaurant A, but the request path
uses Restaurant B. What should happen?

Expected answer:
The ownership-aware Product query finds no match and the API
returns 404 Not Found. No Inventory is created.


Q75. What happens when preparedQuantity is zero or negative?

Expected answer:
@DecimalMin("0.001") validation fails, and the API returns
400 Bad Request with a field-level validation message.


Q76. The application check says no duplicate exists, but the
insert receives a database unique-constraint error. Why?

Expected answer:
Another concurrent transaction inserted the same
Restaurant/Product/date after the check. This demonstrates why
the database constraint is required.


Q77. A client guesses another Inventory UUID. Is UUID secrecy
enough to prevent access?

Expected answer:
No. UUIDs reduce enumeration but are not authorization. The
API must scope lookups by Restaurant and later enforce
authenticated permissions.


Q78. A stale user update fails because the version changed.
Should the server silently retry?

Expected answer:
Not blindly. The application should define conflict semantics,
usually return a conflict response or retry only when the
operation is safely repeatable and business intent remains
valid.


Q79. available + reserved + sold is greater than prepared.
What does this indicate?

Expected answer:
The quantity invariant has been violated. The transaction
should be rejected and investigated rather than persisting
inconsistent Inventory.


Q80. The Product is INACTIVE. Can Inventory be created?

Expected answer:
The current implementation only validates Product existence
and ownership; it does not reject based on ProductStatus.
Adding such a rule would require an explicit business
requirement and must not be invented.

7. FOODSAVER AI ARCHITECTURE AND FUTURE EVOLUTION
============================================================

Q81. Where does Inventory fit in the future FoodSaver AI
workflow?

Expected answer:

Product
   ↓
Inventory
   ↓
Surplus Detection
   ↓
Eligibility / Safety Rules
   ↓
Offer
   ↓
Customer Order

Product defines the catalog item, Inventory provides quantity,
and later modules make their own controlled decisions.


Q82. How can Inventory support surplus detection?

Expected answer:
Inventory provides dated prepared, available, reserved, and
sold quantities. A future surplus module can use authorized
data and time-based rules to identify potential surplus.
Those rules are not implemented yet.


Q83. Does surplus detection determine food safety?

Expected answer:
No. Surplus describes excess or remaining quantity. It does
not mean the food is unsafe or eligible for sale.


Q84. How should eligibility and safety rules relate to AI?

Expected answer:
AI may assist with recommendations and workflow coordination,
but hard food-safety and eligibility rules cannot be bypassed.
Deterministic rules and appropriate qualified oversight remain
authoritative.


Q85. How could demand prediction use Inventory later?

Expected answer:
A future model could use historical prepared and sold
quantities to recommend preparation levels. Recommendations
must not directly mutate authoritative Inventory without
validated business operations.


Q86. How could dynamic pricing use Inventory later?

Expected answer:
A future pricing service could consider eligible available
quantity, time, and demand. Pricing must remain separate from
Inventory accounting and cannot replace safety eligibility.


Q87. How could Offers use Inventory?

Expected answer:
An Offer could reference an eligible quantity derived from
Inventory. Reservation and sale operations would need to
atomically protect quantities from oversubscription.


Q88. How could Customer Orders affect Inventory?

Expected answer:
Order workflows may reserve quantity, release it after
cancellation, and move it to sold after completion. Each
transition must preserve the invariant and be idempotent where
retries are possible.


Q89. Where could Kafka fit later?

Expected answer:
Committed Inventory changes could publish events for surplus,
offers, analytics, or ordering. A reliable design may use an
outbox pattern so database state and event publication do not
diverge. Kafka is not currently implemented.


Q90. Where could Redis fit later?

Expected answer:
Redis could cache read models or support short-lived
coordination where appropriate. Cache invalidation,
Restaurant-scoped keys, consistency, and the database as the
source of truth must be considered. Redis is not currently
implemented.


Q91. How could AI agents participate safely?

Expected answer:
Agents could recommend preparation, identify patterns, or
coordinate workflows. They must call controlled services and
cannot directly bypass quantity invariants, authorization, or
hard food-safety rules.


Q92. What is the most important architecture boundary in this
module?

Expected answer:
Inventory is authoritative operational quantity state, not a
food-safety decision. Later surplus, eligibility, offer, and
order modules must consume it through controlled,
transactional business operations.
