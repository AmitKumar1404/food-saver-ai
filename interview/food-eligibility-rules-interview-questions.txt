============================================================
FOODSAVER AI - FOOD ELIGIBILITY RULES INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
============================================================

Module:
Food Eligibility Rules V1

Purpose:
Interview preparation from beginner to approximately
3-year experience level.

Current implementation:
- Ownership-validated eligibility fact snapshots
- One immutable project-level V1 policy
- Pure deterministic rule evaluation
- Immutable evaluation and rule-result persistence
- Transactional evaluation service
- Ownership-aware REST endpoint with centralized errors
- No AI, RAG, Kafka, Redis, Offer creation, or food-safety
  certification in V1

Current endpoint:
POST /api/v1/restaurants/{restaurantPublicId}/inventory/
{inventoryPublicId}/surplus-detections/{surplusDetectionPublicId}/
eligibility-evaluations


============================================================
1. BEGINNER - MODULE AND SAFETY FUNDAMENTALS
============================================================

Q1. Why did FoodSaver create a separate Food Eligibility
module?

Expected answer:
Surplus Detection answers whether available quantity meets a
configured surplus threshold. Food Eligibility separately
decides whether that snapshot may progress in the platform
workflow. The separation prevents quantity detection from
being confused with eligibility or food-safety certification.


Q2. What is the difference between Surplus Detection and Food
Eligibility?

Expected answer:
Surplus Detection stores a quantity-based historical snapshot
with POTENTIAL_SURPLUS or NOT_SURPLUS. Food Eligibility loads
Restaurant, Product, Inventory, and detection facts, applies
the immutable V1 policy and deterministic rules, then stores
ELIGIBLE_FOR_OFFER, NOT_ELIGIBLE, or REQUIRES_REVIEW.


Q3. Why is POTENTIAL_SURPLUS not equivalent to food-safety
approval?

Expected answer:
POTENTIAL_SURPLUS only means availableQuantity met the surplus
threshold used by the detection module. It says nothing about
regulatory compliance, handling conditions, suitability for
consumption, or approval for sale.


Q4. Why should AI not be the final food-safety authority?

Expected answer:
AI output can be probabilistic, incomplete, or unsupported by
the applicable authority. FoodSaver keeps deterministic
controls and qualified human oversight authoritative. AI must
not certify safety or bypass a blocking rule.


Q5. Why are deterministic rules evaluated before introducing
Agentic AI?

Expected answer:
Deterministic rules are reproducible, testable, auditable, and
tied to an immutable policy version. They establish hard
workflow boundaries before any future agent is allowed to
recommend or coordinate actions.


Q6. Explain the BLOCK > REVIEW > PASS precedence.

Expected answer:
If any rule returns BLOCK, the overall status is
NOT_ELIGIBLE. Otherwise, any REVIEW produces
REQUIRES_REVIEW. Only when every rule passes does the result
become ELIGIBLE_FOR_OFFER.


Q7. Why does the evaluator run all applicable rules instead of
stopping at the first failure?

Expected answer:
FoodSaver persists every rule result for auditability. Running
all rules preserves the complete decision evidence, including
multiple blocks, reviews, and passes, while overall precedence
still determines one final status.


Q8. What is an immutable policy version?

Expected answer:
Once published, its identity and meaning do not change. A
policy correction requires a new version instead of rewriting
the policy that historical evaluations used.


============================================================
2. INTERMEDIATE - POLICY, OWNERSHIP, AND HISTORY
============================================================

Q9. Why does an evaluation store policy key, version, and
source reference?

Expected answer:
The fields identify the exact project policy family, immutable
version, and governance source used. They make a historical
decision explainable without exposing internal database IDs.


Q10. Why is the policy effectiveFrom configuration required?

Expected answer:
The policy's effective timestamp is governance metadata and
must be supplied explicitly through
foodsaver.food-eligibility.policy.effective-from. It must not
be generated from evaluation time.


Q11. Why is there no arbitrary or default policy timestamp?

Expected answer:
A generated default could make the application apply a policy
from an unapproved point in time and undermine historical
explainability. Missing or invalid configuration therefore
fails startup validation.


Q12. What happens when policy information is missing?

Expected answer:
Invalid required V1 configuration fails startup. At the rule
domain level, a missing supplied policy produces REVIEW, but
the current fixed V1 resolver always provides its validated
single policy. No artificial production REVIEW condition is
invented.


Q13. Why is ownership validation important?

Expected answer:
Public UUIDs are not authority by themselves. FoodSaver must
prove that the Inventory belongs to the requested Restaurant,
its Product belongs to the same Restaurant, and the Surplus
Detection belongs to that Inventory before evaluating facts.


Q14. Why use ownership-aware repository queries?

Expected answer:
They scope lookup and authorization together so a resource
owned by another Restaurant is returned as not found. This
reduces accidental cross-tenant data disclosure.


Q15. Why are multiple evaluations allowed for one Surplus
Detection?

Expected answer:
Evaluations are immutable history. Re-evaluation may be needed
after a policy version or authoritative fact changes, so
surplus_detection_id is indexed but not unique.


Q16. Why persist rule-level results?

Expected answer:
The final status alone cannot explain which checks passed,
blocked, or required review. Rule code, outcome, reason code,
and message provide stable audit evidence.


Q17. Why is @Transactional used on evaluation persistence?

Expected answer:
One service transaction covers loading the snapshot, saving
the parent evaluation, and saving every child rule result.
Callers must not observe a parent without its complete audit
results.


Q18. What does atomic persistence mean here?

Expected answer:
The evaluation and all rule-result rows commit together, or
none of them commit. A partial eligibility history is not a
valid outcome.


============================================================
3. ADVANCED - PERSISTENCE, API, AND ERROR CONTRACTS
============================================================

Q19. How does rollback work if rule-result persistence fails?

Expected answer:
The failure propagates as a runtime exception through the
transactional service proxy. Spring marks the transaction for
rollback, so the earlier evaluation insert is not committed.
The integration test verifies this against the real database.


Q20. Why use public UUIDs instead of exposing database IDs?

Expected answer:
Long IDs are internal persistence details and are easy to
enumerate. UUID public IDs provide stable external identity
without exposing table sequencing; authorization checks are
still required.


Q21. Why are JPA entities not returned by the controller?

Expected answer:
Entities expose persistence concerns, lazy relationships, and
internal IDs. Dedicated response DTOs define the supported API
contract and return only approved snapshot information.


Q22. Why are entity relationships LAZY?

Expected answer:
An evaluation or rule-result query should not automatically
load the complete Restaurant, Product, Inventory, and history
graph. LAZY relationships avoid unnecessary queries and large
object graphs; services load what their transaction needs.


Q23. Why does NOT_ELIGIBLE return HTTP 201 instead of 4xx?

Expected answer:
The request succeeded and created a valid immutable evaluation
snapshot. NOT_ELIGIBLE is the business outcome, not an HTTP
request failure.


Q24. Why does REQUIRES_REVIEW return HTTP 201?

Expected answer:
It is also a completed evaluation outcome that blocks
automatic progression. The current fixed V1 policy has no
legitimate production review condition, but the API and domain
contract preserve the state for a future approved condition.


Q25. How should malformed UUID path parameters be handled?

Expected answer:
MethodArgumentTypeMismatchException is handled centrally. The
API returns HTTP 400 with the existing ErrorResponse fields:
timestamp, status, message, request path, and a field-level
error such as restaurantPublicId must be a valid UUID.


Q26. Why should ErrorResponse handling be centralized?

Expected answer:
A RestControllerAdvice keeps controllers focused on HTTP
orchestration and gives validation, malformed paths, and
not-found failures one safe, consistent response contract
without repeated try/catch blocks.


============================================================
4. 3-YEAR EXPERIENCE - FUTURE ARCHITECTURE
============================================================

Q27. What role could RAG have in the future architecture?

Expected answer:
RAG could retrieve policy explanations or evidence from
approved sources to assist reviewers. It is not part of V1 and
must not become the authority that changes deterministic rule
outcomes.


Q28. Why can RAG not override deterministic hard controls?

Expected answer:
Retrieved text can be stale, ambiguous, or incorrectly
matched. Authoritative versioned controls remain the enforced
boundary; RAG may explain them but cannot convert BLOCK or
REVIEW into PASS.


Q29. How could Kafka be introduced later?

Expected answer:
After transaction and event contracts are designed, FoodSaver
could publish an eligibility-evaluated event for downstream
workflows. An outbox or equivalent reliability pattern would
be needed so database commit and event publication cannot
silently diverge.


Q30. How could Redis be introduced later?

Expected answer:
Redis could cache immutable policy lookups or safe read models
after ownership, invalidation, and consistency rules are
defined. It must not become an unversioned authority or bypass
the database audit record.


Q31. What is a safe way to introduce an AI agent into this
flow?

Expected answer:
Give it narrowly scoped tools after deterministic evaluation,
require authorization and audit, and limit it to explanation,
coordination, or recommendations. It cannot mutate Inventory,
create Offers automatically, certify safety, or override hard
controls.

Possible follow-up:
Should the agent write directly to eligibility tables?

Follow-up answer:
No. Eligibility snapshots must be produced through the
validated deterministic service and transaction boundary.


Q32. Which parts of the current implementation are
intentionally not AI-driven?

Expected answer:
Ownership lookup, fact construction, policy resolution,
lifecycle checks, status precedence, persistence, transaction
rollback, and HTTP error mapping are deterministic. V1 has no
AI, RAG, Kafka, Redis, Offer, or Order integration.


============================================================
5. SCENARIO, FOLLOW-UP, AND TRICKY QUESTIONS
============================================================

Scenario:
A Surplus Detection is POTENTIAL_SURPLUS, but the Restaurant
is PENDING_VERIFICATION. What is the outcome?

Answer:
The Restaurant lifecycle rule returns BLOCK, so the overall
result is NOT_ELIGIBLE. This is a platform-lifecycle decision,
not a statement that the food is unsafe.


Tricky question:
If six rules PASS and one rule BLOCKS, can the result be
ELIGIBLE_FOR_OFFER because most rules passed?

Answer:
No. BLOCK has highest precedence. Rule evaluation is not a
vote or score.


Scenario:
The evaluation row is inserted, then saving rule results
fails. What should remain in the database?

Answer:
Neither the evaluation nor rule results should remain. The
transaction rolls back atomically.


Follow-up:
Does a public UUID remove the need for ownership validation?

Answer:
No. UUIDs reduce enumeration risk but do not grant access.
Every lookup must still enforce the Restaurant ownership
chain.
